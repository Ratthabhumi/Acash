SOURCE STATUS: EXTERNAL / UNVERIFIED
THIS FILE PRESERVES CLAIMS; IT DOES NOT ENDORSE THEM.

## 1. Source Context & Raw Claims
- **Source Medium**: Retail social media / trading video.
- **Narrative**: Promotional framing presenting an "easy/every day" mechanical trading strategy based on the New York market open.
- **Reported Rule Set**:
  1. Mark the 9:30 high/low of the first opening candle (09:30 America/New_York).
  2. Wait for a candle close above the high (long) or below the low (short) as breakout confirmation.
  3. Claimed target: Fixed 1:2 RR (Risk-to-Reward ratio).
  4. Variant: Entry upon the creation and retest of a 1-minute FVG formed during the opening expansion.

## 2. Human-Reviewed Scientific Critique
- **Unverified Assertions**: The claim that this pattern occurs "every day" or offers an "easy" edge is classic retail promotional hyperbole (`EXTERNAL_UNVERIFIED_CLAIM`).
- **Fixed Risk-to-Reward Fallacy**: Claiming a fixed 1:2 RR does not ensure positive expectancy. Win rate and realized reward-to-risk are interdependent and conditioned by execution friction.
- **Coarse-Bar & Intrabar Path Ambiguity**: Fails to account for whether stop or target was triggered first during high-volatility opening minutes.
- **Literature Alignment**: FVG edge is unsupported by mature peer-reviewed literature; contemporary preprints show conflicting evidence with edge collapsing after spread and commission.
