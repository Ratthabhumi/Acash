# ACASH Operator Decision Charter v1

## 1. Document Purpose & Scope

This charter exists to preserve decision discipline when excitement, fear, social pressure, ambition, sunk cost, novelty, or short-term results might otherwise distort judgment.

It is a behavioral governance aid.

It is NOT:
- a trading strategy
- scientific evidence
- a personality diagnosis
- an investment mandate
- an authorization mechanism
- a substitute for empirical research
- a source of paper/live trading authority

When this charter conflicts with:
- empirical evidence
- sealed ACASH research governance
- risk controls
- legal/compliance obligations
- explicit capital authority

the stronger formal control wins.

---

## 2. Core Operating Loop

The operator adheres to this durable operational progression:

$$\text{LEARN DEEPLY} \longrightarrow \text{BUILD SOMETHING REAL} \longrightarrow \text{CONNECT IT TO REAL PEOPLE} \longrightarrow \text{EXPOSE IT TO THE REAL WORLD}$$
$$\longrightarrow \text{MEASURE} \longrightarrow \text{ADAPT} \longrightarrow \text{SCALE ONLY AFTER EVIDENCE} \longrightarrow \text{REPEAT}$$

### A. Learn Deeply
Build real expertise in a limited number of important domains. Do not confuse consuming information with competence. Depth should eventually produce usable judgment, not endless preparation.

### B. Build Something Real
Ideas must become outputs: software, research, systems, products, experiments, documentation, services, or other verifiable work. Shipping creates evidence.

### C. Connect It to Real People
Avoid operating permanently in isolation. Seek: users, customers, collaborators, mentors, reviewers, and domain experts. Real value must survive contact with people outside the creator's own head.

### D. Expose It to the Real World
Do not hide good work indefinitely. Use appropriate demonstrations, documentation, GitHub repositories, research reports, public artifacts, user feedback, or market feedback. Distribution is different from competence; both can matter.

### E. Measure
Define what success and failure mean before interpreting outcomes. Prefer observable evidence, predefined metrics, logs, tests, reproducible records, and external feedback. Do not substitute confidence or excitement for evidence.

### F. Adapt
When evidence contradicts the current method: do not defend the method merely because effort has already been invested. Preserve useful evidence. Identify the failure mode. Change the method only under the appropriate governance process.

### G. Scale Only After Evidence
Do not scale capital, complexity, automation, team size, public claims, or operational risk before the underlying process has demonstrated sufficient reliability. Scale sequence should generally be:

$$\text{Individual Work} \longrightarrow \text{Repeatable Process} \longrightarrow \text{Automation} \longrightarrow \text{Delegation / Team} \longrightarrow \text{Broader Deployment}$$

Proceed to the next stage only when justified by empirical records.

### H. Repeat
Re-enter the cycle continuously with accumulated empirical findings and refined models.

## 3. Decision Guardrails

### Evidence Over Narrative
A compelling story is not evidence. Social-media popularity, confidence, status, authority claims, aesthetic charts, or one successful outcome must not override reproducible evidence.

### Depth Without Isolation
Deep independent thinking is valuable. But prolonged isolation can prevent feedback, error correction, distribution, and real-world validation. Before concluding that an idea is good, seek appropriate external reality checks.

### Decisiveness Without Impulsiveness
Avoid endless indecision. But speed alone is not a virtue. For material decisions:
1. Identify the decision,
2. Identify available evidence,
3. Identify downside,
4. Identify reversibility,
5. Then decide.

### Ambition With Guardrails
Ambition for achievement, money, recognition, ownership, or scale is acceptable. It must not justify:
- shortcuts,
- fabricated evidence,
- hidden risk,
- excess leverage,
- governance bypass,
- premature deployment, or
- changing rules after seeing results.

### No Status-Chasing
Do not choose projects primarily because they look impressive. Prefer real competence, real utility, real ownership, real users, and real results. Recognition should be treated as a possible consequence, not the primary evidence of value.

### Sunk Cost Is Not Evidence
Past effort does not make a failing approach correct. When evidence materially invalidates an approach: preserve the record, learn from it, and stop or redesign under proper governance.

### Collaboration Without Approval-Seeking
Collaboration is valuable. External approval is not the same as correctness. Do not:
- agree merely to avoid disagreement,
- change conclusions to please others, or
- continue weak work because others are emotionally invested.

### One Primary Objective at a Time
Avoid uncontrolled project proliferation. New ideas may enter an intake queue, but not every interesting idea becomes an active project. Finish, reject, park, or explicitly pause existing commitments before creating uncontrolled parallel scope.

## 4. ACASH Application

The following rules apply especially strongly to ACASH:

1. **Governance beats enthusiasm**: No exciting strategy, creator claim, research paper, AI suggestion, or short-term result overrides sealed governance.
2. **External claims enter intake first**: A new idea must first be classified as a source claim, mechanism, hypothesis candidate, or control. It must not jump directly to implementation.
3. **The implementation agent does not choose the science**: AI coding agents may implement, test, document, verify, and report. They may NOT independently decide hypotheses, economic theses, parameter choices, research universes, acceptance thresholds, or capital allocations.
4. **Do not rescue failed hypotheses**: A failed or unsupported hypothesis remains part of the historical evidence. Do not repeatedly modify it until it becomes profitable. A materially new mechanism requires a new hypothesis lineage.
5. **Every empirical variation counts**: Failed, deleted, AI-generated, human-generated, and unpublished variations remain part of the trial history count ($K$).
6. **Costs and execution are part of the strategy**: Spread, slippage, fees, market impact, financing, borrow, roll, and execution ambiguity must not be treated as optional decorations.
7. **Ambiguous evidence fails closed**: If evidence cannot establish the required fact, the correct state is `UNKNOWN`, `BLOCKED`, or `NOT_AUTHORIZED`. Never fill missing evidence with optimism.
8. **Historical performance does not authorize capital**:
   $$\text{Research Result} \neq \text{Paper Authorization}$$
   $$\text{Paper Eligibility} \neq \text{Paper Authorization}$$
   $$\text{Paper Success} \neq \text{Live Authorization}$$
   $$\text{Live Eligibility} \neq \text{Capital Authorization}$$
9. **Real capital is separate from simulated accounting capital**: Simulated AUM exists only for normalized research accounting. It must never be represented as real money.
10. **Preserve downside before pursuing upside**: Avoid irreversible actions when a reversible path is available.

## 5. Override Check

Before accepting an unusual or emotionally driven request, ask:

- **A.** Is this request motivated by new evidence, or merely excitement / fear / impatience / status pressure?
- **B.** Does it contradict an existing sealed rule?
- **C.** Does it change a metric, threshold, dataset, parameter, or hypothesis after outcome information has been observed?
- **D.** Does it increase irreversible risk?
- **E.** Is the same objective achievable through a reversible, read-only, shadow, or separately branched action?
- **F.** Would this decision still seem reasonable if the most recent result had gone the opposite direction?
- **G.** Are we confusing:
  - simulation with money,
  - correlation with causation,
  - a source claim with evidence, or
  - attention with value?

If any answer raises a governance concern:
- **DO NOT silently comply.**
- Surface the conflict explicitly.
- Offer the safest governance-consistent alternative.
- The human retains final authority, but the system must make the trade-off visible.

## 6. Stop Conditions

Stop and reassess when:
- scope expands without explicit authorization
- a project is being changed primarily to rescue prior poor results
- metrics are changed after seeing outcomes
- source authority is unclear
- capital assumptions are confused with real funds
- implementation begins before the scientific question is defined
- a social-media claim is being treated as evidence
- repeated retries are being used to obtain a desired result
- sunk cost becomes the main justification for continuing
- urgency is being used to bypass verification
- a decision depends on unknown data that can be verified first

---

## 7. Positive Operating Principles

- Learn deeply.
- Ship real work.
- Seek reality, not validation.
- Collaborate without surrendering judgment.
- Measure before interpreting.
- Change course when evidence requires it.
- Do not confuse ambition with urgency.
- Do not confuse visibility with value.
- Do not confuse complexity with sophistication.
- Do not scale what has not been validated.
- Preserve evidence, including failure.
- Prefer reversible decisions when uncertainty is high.
- Keep capital authority explicit.
- Finish or formally park work before uncontrolled expansion.

---

## 8. Charter Is Not Dogma

This charter is itself subject to evidence and deliberate revision.

It must NOT be used to:
- suppress legitimate new ideas
- justify excessive caution forever
- prevent experimentation
- substitute process for judgment
- block rational risk taking

If a principle proves harmful or incomplete, it may be amended deliberately through documented governance. Do not silently reinterpret it.
